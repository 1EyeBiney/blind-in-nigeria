# Checking the site against the study documents

Written 28 October 2026 after reading the ministry folder's Nigeria series (notes 10 through 40 and the sourced research passes NG1 to NG10, T1, U1 to U3), the broad research series (R1 to R7, N1 to N4), the partner vetting report and its Bethesda research, the cane pipeline note and its research, the story documents and their verification, and the Bethesda correspondence including the representative's visit report of 25 October and Bethesda's food figures of 26 October. Not read: the fee-checking passes V1 to V4, the story research L1 to L4, the Pacelli-specific vetting passes, the due-diligence standards pass, the RCCG research, the shipping and supply pricing passes, and the church process documents (00 to 09, ideas, scoring, leadership). None of those bear on a claim the site makes today, except that L1 to L4 are the natural sources for the begging study.

This document names no private person. Anyone named in the ministry folder who is not a public figure in a public role is referred to by role here.

## 1. Verdict in brief

The site's figures hold. Every money figure traces to the feeding analysis, which traces to Bethesda's own quantities of 26 October and to online Lagos prices with URLs. The headcounts trace to the visit report. No number on the site contradicts a source.

The site's words are looser than its figures. Six sentences say more than the sources say, and one word, "child," is wrong often enough to matter. None of these is a scandal; all are the kind of drift that happens when a page is written from a brief rather than from the documents. The fixes are listed in section 3 and are small.

The study documents hold a great deal the site could use, with public sources, for the pages it already promises. Section 5 says which future studies could be written now and from what.

### The numbers

Every estimate traces to the feeding analysis and through it to the food letter and the price listings; the arithmetic was re-checked when the data file was built. Three notes:

- The exchange rate. The data file dates the 1,330 rate to 27 October and calls it "observed during ministry planning". The documented observation is the Central Bank rate of about 1,330 on 22 to 25 September 2026, with the Lagos street rate about 1,390 and Bethesda using 1,400 in its own letters. The feeding analysis of 26 October reused 1,330 without a fresh check. The rate row should carry the date it was actually observed, or be re-observed and dated.
- The counts table labels 472 as "students and residents together". See the 472 note above; the label should match what Bethesda said and say the breakdown is being clarified.
- The goal's description in goals.json says "down to the cost per child". Same fix as above.

## 4. What the site can use now, with public sources

These facts are in the research passes with URLs and dates accessed, and name no private person.

- Nigeria's only national eye survey (2005 to 2007): 4.2 percent of adults over 40 blind, about 1.13 million people at the time; cataract 43 percent of blindness, glaucoma 17 percent, corneal scarring 8 percent, trachoma 4 percent; 84 percent avoidable; the southwest the lowest region at 2.8 percent. The verification notes flag two corrections: the southwest is not the zone with the highest cataract share, and the CDC comparison should use 0.21 to 0.43 percent blindness for Americans of the same ages, not 0.8 percent.
- Saki East, Oyo State, 2022 survey of adults over 50: 1.7 percent blind, 94.6 percent avoidable. 2024 survey of 3,671 schoolchildren: half of impairment from uncorrected refractive error; eye injury the leading cause of actual blindness in children.
- Glaucoma: about 5 percent of adults over 40; only 5.6 percent of those who have it know.
- Reliable electricity for about 13 percent of Nigerians (Afrobarometer AD814, 2024).
- Screen readers: NVDA free; JAWS reported at about ₦900,000 in Nigeria (Foundation for Investigative Journalism, 2024); no Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo NVDA interface and no eSpeak Yoruba voice; a 2026 study in which five blind Nigerian students preferred braille to Yoruba speech because of tone errors.
- Phones and data: budget Android phones ₦100,000 to ₦200,000; monthly data ₦3,000 to ₦7,500 for 7 to 20 GB (2026 guides).
- Money: the World Bank's measured average cost of sending money from the US to Nigeria, 2.72 percent (Q3 2025); registering a Nigerian nonprofit costs roughly ₦115,000 to ₦205,000 and takes six to eight weeks; SCUML registration is free.
- Canes: the Lagos listings above; a 2023 Tribune report of ₦15,000 to ₦20,000 blamed on import costs; no Nigerian cane manufacturer found; Bethesda's own account that potholes and public buses wear canes out.
- Organizations serving blind Nigerians, with public contact pages: Accesstech Innovation (Lagos; NVDA training; also the Konga cane seller), the Anglo-Nigerian Welfare Association for the Blind (Yaba), the Federal Nigeria Society for the Blind (Oshodi), the Resource Centre for the Blind (Yaba), Hope for the Blind (Zaria, Christian), the Nigeria Association of the Blind, the Oyo State Agency for Persons with Disabilities. Also Pacelli School for the Blind, Surulere, as a public institution, without any assessment.
- Bethesda's public record: founded by Rev. Dr. Chioma Ohakwe after her brothers were blinded by untreated measles during the civil war; care began in the late 1990s, formal establishment 2001, residential from 2005, moved to its present site in 2011; a congregation since 2020 in which the pastor, musicians and most worshippers are blind; funded by donations and the residents' crafts and music. Sources: Bethesda's own site and the Guardian Nigeria interview of 17 March 2018, plus Legit.ng coverage of the church.

## 9. Answers and follow-up, 28 October 2026

- No gift has been sent to Bethesda. The home page and ledger are correct as they stand. The representative's fee and expenses are Brian's own obligation, not part of the charitable effort, and are not referenced on the site.
- Published, attributed press interviews with named blind Nigerians may be quoted on the site. The begging and Ibadan studies can be written from L1 and NG4 with names as the press printed them.
- The "70 percent of blind people in Nigeria beg" sentence: no document in the ministry folder states it. The nearest figures are (a) the 2010 Ilorin study in the Middle East African Journal of Ophthalmology, in which 77 percent of 61 totally blind adults in one city supported themselves by street begging (L1, story 16, verification-story), and (b) the same paper's citation of an older count that about 70 percent of roughly 4,000 beggars in Lagos were blind, a statement about beggars, not about blind people (found in the paper itself, not in the folder's notes). The folder's own summaries say "most blind adults without family support survive by begging" (handout 17, script 18) and "begging remains a common survival strategy for blind adults nationally" (NG4) without a national percentage. A 90 percent figure in the folder refers to avoidable blindness, not begging.
