# White Canes for Bethesda: Collect Here, Buy There, or Both

Prepared October 5, 2026, from two research passes (logistics/research/C1 and C2). Both passes were thinner than hoped because many vendor and agency pages could not be read by machine; what follows is what was confirmed, what was not, and what it suggests.

## The organization Brian remembered

No organization called Canes for Christ, or any spelling of it, was found. The group that matches the description is Global Cane Outreach, a volunteer-run 501(c)(3) in Scotts Valley, California, founded in 2010, which equips Christian teams traveling abroad with white canes, audio Bibles, and a short mobility-training course, and lists Ghana, Liberia, Ethiopia, South Africa, and Kenya among its countries. Nigeria is not on the list. Its website does not say whether the canes are new or donated or how a church would send canes to it; its published contact is info@globalcaneoutreach.org and (831) 216-8122. It does publish two things of direct use: a "Mobility Training 101" course for lay teams and a white-cane repair video, with a sizing rule for beginners (ground to armpit).

## The best model found

You Cane Give Initiative, Nashville, is the closest thing to a working template for what Brian is imagining. It collects used and even broken canes from blind people in the United States, repairs them (tips, elastic, joints), sorts them, and sends them abroad with volunteer trainers who teach people to use them; it reports more than two thousand canes delivered in over thirty countries. Nigeria is not named. Its model has the three features the church would need to copy: it accepts used canes, which no vendor program does; it pairs every cane with instruction, because a cane handed over without training is a stick; and it travels with people rather than freight. The one documented small-scale precedent reinforces the last point: an Idaho family hand-carried about forty donated canes to Ethiopia in a single suitcase, bringing a pipe cutter to shorten them on arrival.

Things that do not work for this purpose, so no time is spent on them: the NFB Free White Cane Program is for US residents only; no Lions cane-recycling program that exports canes was found (Lions in Accra bought new canes locally, which is itself a hint); and Bosma Enterprises and Easterseals Crossroads in Indianapolis have no cane donation or recycling program that could be found, though neither was asked directly.

## Nigeria as a source

This is the finding that changes the plan. White canes are sold in Nigeria, and the seller is one we already know. Konga lists a "White Mobility Cane for the Blind," 125 cm, four-section folding aluminum, at ₦12,000 (about $9), sold by Accesstech Innovation Nigeria, the Lagos organization that teaches blind people NVDA and that the October 2 note identified as the closest match to Brian's own work; the listing gives a bulk-order phone line. Jumia lists a mobility cane at ₦19,500 (about $15). A 2023 Nigerian press report put canes at ₦15,000 to ₦20,000 and blamed import costs, which fits: these are imported canes sold locally, not Nigerian-made, and no Nigerian manufacturer or workshop was found. State governments do hand out canes on White Cane Day (a hundred in Abia, two hundred collapsible canes in Abuja), with no price or source published.

Against that, the cheapest new folding cane found in the United States was about $26 at Maxi-Aids, and the NFB cane is $50, before any shipping or customs. So a cane bought in Lagos costs a third of a cane bought here, needs no freight, carries no customs risk, is the right length range already, and comes from an organization Brian wants a relationship with anyway. On import duty, nothing definite was found: no tariff line for walking aids for the disabled could be read, the 2018 Disability Act provides for the national commission to procure assistive devices but contains no import exemption, and a clearing agent's page says donated goods can clear duty-free for a registered nonprofit with paperwork but does not name disability equipment. For the December trunk this is moot, since friends in Ibadan move goods through their own channel; for anything larger it would need a Lagos clearing agent's answer.

## What this suggests for the church's cane pipeline

Two streams, not one.

For quantity, buy in Lagos. A gift of ₦480,000 (about $360) buys forty canes from Accesstech at the Konga price, delivered in Lagos, and a phone call to their bulk line may do better. Bethesda receives them in a week, not in December, and the money also puts the church in front of Accesstech as a customer before Brian ever asks them about remote teaching. Our representative can make the call, confirm the price, and arrange delivery to Surulere with a receipt, which is exactly the kind of job a representative on the ground is for. This is also the honest answer to the question of where the budget goes: canes bought here would eat most of the $1,000; canes bought there leave room for everything else.

For meaning, collect here. Used canes gathered at the two Indiana conferences this month, repaired with new tips and elastic, and carried in a suitcase by someone who can teach, follow the You Cane Give pattern and give the congregation and the blind community in Indiana something to hold and give. Thirty folding canes weigh under fifteen pounds and fit in the December bag, or travel with whoever first visits Lagos. They are a story and a gift, not a supply chain, and should be described that way.

What the collection needs to work: a bin and a sign at each conference asking for spare or retired canes, folding preferred; a repair afternoon with replacement tips (rolling-ball and pencil, $7 to $12 each from the US vendors, or, cheaper, bought in Lagos with the new canes) and elastic cord; sorting by length with a tape measure, keeping 44 to 54 inches and setting aside the rest; a count for Bethesda; and a note that each cane came from a blind Hoosier who no longer needs it.

## Calls worth making

You Cane Give Initiative, Nashville, to ask how they inspect, repair, sort, and pack, whether they have ever sent to Nigeria, and whether they would take Indiana's surplus if the church does not. Global Cane Outreach, for the training course and repair guidance and to ask whether Nigeria is on their horizon. Accesstech Innovation's bulk line, by our representative, for a quoted price on forty canes delivered to Bethesda. And one Lagos clearing agent, only if a shipment larger than the trunk is ever contemplated.

## What was not found

Any organization that takes used canes for export other than You Cane Give; any US church that has shipped canes to Nigeria; Nigerian cane manufacturing; the tariff rate on walking aids; cane donation programs at Indiana's blind-services agencies; and current prices from Jiji or Lagos shops. The research files list every source and every page that could not be read.
