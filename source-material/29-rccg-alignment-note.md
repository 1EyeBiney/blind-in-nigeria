# Bethesda's Church Affiliation: The Redeemed Christian Church of God

A note for the leadership of Brian's church, September 30, 2026. On September 27 the founder of Bethesda Home for the Blind, Lagos, wrote that its congregation "currently worships with the Redeemed Christian Church of God." This note describes that church plainly, from its own published statements and from reputable coverage, so leadership can judge alignment for itself. Research file P7 in the partners folder carries the sources.

## What RCCG is

The Redeemed Christian Church of God was founded in Lagos in 1952 by Josiah Akindayomi and has been led since 1981 by Enoch Adeboye, a former mathematics lecturer whom members call "Daddy G.O." It is one of the largest churches in Africa, Pentecostal in doctrine and worship, and by its own count has more than nine million members in about 50,000 parishes across nearly two hundred countries. It runs a university, a Bible college, a large campground called Redemption City on the Lagos–Ibadan road, media outlets, and a charity arm. Its stated aim is to plant a parish within five minutes' walk of everyone in the developing world and five minutes' drive in the developed world. In North America it operates as RCCG North America, with somewhere between one hundred and eight hundred parishes depending on the source; public listings show at least two in Indianapolis, so a member of the church could attend an RCCG service locally and form a first-hand impression.

## What it believes

RCCG parishes publish a common statement of faith. It affirms the divine inspiration and infallibility of the Bible; the Trinity; the deity, virgin birth, sinless life, crucifixion, resurrection, ascension, and return of Christ; salvation through repentance and faith in Christ's finished work; sanctification as a life of holiness; water baptism by immersion and the Lord's Supper; the second coming, a rapture and tribulation, a millennial reign, and heaven and hell as literal places. On all of that, an independent Bible church would find familiar ground.

The points where it differs from a non-charismatic evangelical church are these. It teaches the baptism of the Holy Spirit as a distinct experience evidenced by speaking in tongues. It teaches divine healing through the atonement; one secondary source describes this as healing "without medicine," but that phrase was not found in the church's own text, and for a charity serving blind people it is worth asking about directly. Its mission language ("to make heaven, and to take as many people with us") and its emphasis on holiness as a lifestyle are strong and explicit. And it is widely associated with prosperity teaching: Lifeway Research, an evangelical body, cautioned in 2021 that RCCG has prosperity-gospel elements and Word of Faith connections, and advised evangelicals considering contact to talk directly with the clergy involved rather than assume either way. Adeboye once said that people who do not tithe could not reach heaven and later publicly withdrew the statement as unbiblical. RCCG's own wording on tithing and prosperity could not be retrieved for this note.

## Reputation and relationships

RCCG is described in serious coverage as the wealthiest and most popular Pentecostal church in Nigeria, funded largely by educated young professionals and defined by missionary expansion. Its leader has drawn criticism in Nigeria at various points: for a 2021 prayer that others would "bow" to RCCG members, for a 2024 memo restricting outside preachers in its pulpits, and in 2026 for what critics called silence on national hardship, to which the church replied that he is a clergyman and not a politician. No financial scandal was found in the sources checked. RCCG sits inside the mainstream of Nigerian Christianity: Adeboye served as president of the Pentecostal Fellowship of Nigeria, and RCCG belongs to national church councils in at least one Western country. Whether American non-Pentecostal churches commonly partner with RCCG congregations is not documented; the honest answer is that it happens case by case, and Lifeway's counsel is caution and conversation, not refusal.

## What this means for the church

Bethesda's own public materials do not mention RCCG or any denomination, so the affiliation rests on what its founder told us. Three readings are possible, and the next email should ask which is true: the home's in-house Sunday service, where everyone from the pastor to the choir is blind, is itself an RCCG parish; the home's residents attend a nearby RCCG parish; or the founder is RCCG and the home's service follows its pattern without formal standing. The difference matters for one practical question, whether RCCG has any authority over the home or its money, and for one spiritual question, what is preached to the blind students on Sunday.

Stated neutrally: a gift to Bethesda's school or home carries no faith condition on the giver, and the church would not be affiliating with RCCG by giving. It would be supporting a blind-led Christian charity whose worship is Pentecostal and whose broader movement carries prosperity associations that the church may or may not be comfortable near. On the essentials of the gospel there is agreement; on tongues, healing, and money there are differences that leadership can weigh on their own terms. It is worth noting that Bethesda's own conduct toward us so far, answering every question, welcoming canes and Scripture and free teaching, and asking for nothing, does not look like a prosperity ministry in practice.

## Questions to ask Bethesda before partnering

Which of the three readings above describes the Sunday service, and which RCCG parish or province, if any, oversees it. Whether the home's governance and finances are separate from the congregation and who oversees the home. How healing is taught, and whether students are encouraged to seek medical care, including eye care. Whether donors, families, or students are asked to give in expectation of blessing. Whether a gift for care and education would carry any doctrinal condition on either side. And whether both sides are content to work together across differences on tongues and eschatology, with a plain way to raise disagreements.
