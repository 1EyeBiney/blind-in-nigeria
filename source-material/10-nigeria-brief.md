# Serving the blind in Nigeria: research brief

A research brief written September 15, 2026, for the research passes that produced "Serving the Blind in Nigeria." The church ministry options document was paused; this stream asks a wider question: what does it actually take to serve blind people in Nigeria well, what is already there, and where is the need greatest, especially in Oyo State, so that a small Indiana church with a family connection to Ibadan, a blind instructor who teaches screen readers remotely, and a few thousand dollars a year can put its effort where it counts. The answer may lie beyond teaching technology; sight restoration, education, rehabilitation, livelihoods, advocacy, and faith-based work are all in scope.

## What we already know (from research files R2 and N3)

Nigeria's national blindness survey found cataract responsible for about 43 percent of blindness nationally and 47 percent in the rainforest zone that includes the southwest; glaucoma about 17 percent; corneal scarring 8 percent; trachoma 4 percent; river blindness 1 percent; and 84 percent of blindness avoidable or treatable. The southwest had the lowest regional prevalence. A 2024 systematic review put childhood blindness at about 0.3 percent and visual impairment at about 7 percent of Nigerian children, cataract leading. Reliable electricity reaches about 13 percent of Nigerians. NVDA is free; JAWS's cheapest home license is about $90 a year. Institutions already identified: University College Hospital Ibadan (ophthalmology), the Federal College of Education (Special) in Oyo town, the Nigeria Association of the Blind (Oyo chapter unconfirmed), Sightsavers and CBM in Nigeria, Pacelli School for the Blind in Lagos, the Anglo-Nigerian Welfare Association for the Blind, and a Gindiri centre for the blind in Plateau State. Bank-to-bank transfers to Nigeria cost about 2.7 percent; registering a small Nigerian nonprofit costs about $45 to $60 and takes six to eight weeks.

## Geography

Oyo State first: Ibadan (the capital and largest city), Oyo town, Ogbomoso, Saki, and the rural local government areas. Nigeria as context. Ghana noted only where a second site would matter.

## Report template

Title. Bottom line (three to five sentences). Findings (numbered, sourced). Oyo State specifically (what is there, what is missing). What it would cost to help (concrete unit costs where they exist: one surgery, one year of school, one white cane, one device). Where a small church fits (honest: where it could help, where it should not try). Organizations to contact (name, what they do, city, public contact page). Could not confirm. Sources.

## The seven passes

NG1, the people and the causes. Prevalence of blindness and visual impairment in Nigeria and in the southwest and Oyo State; causes by age group; childhood blindness; glaucoma's burden in Yoruba populations; any survey newer than 2005 to 2007, including the Saki East survey of 2022 and any rapid assessment of avoidable blindness in Oyo; what harmattan dust and air quality actually do to eyes; poverty and rural-urban patterns; how many blind people that implies for Ibadan and Oyo State. Write to nigeria/research/NG1-people-and-causes.md.

NG2, eye care and sight restoration. Who provides eye care in Oyo State and at what cost: University College Hospital Ibadan, Eleta Eye Institute in Ibadan, LAUTECH Teaching Hospital in Ogbomoso, the Oyo State Hospitals Management Board eye units, private clinics, outreach cataract camps run by Sightsavers, CBM, Lions, the Tulsi Chanrai Foundation, and others; the cost of a cataract surgery in Nigeria and what patients actually pay; glaucoma detection and treatment access; the national eye health programme and any state plan; the surgical rate and backlog; whether a small foreign donor can sponsor surgeries through an established provider and at what price per sight restored. Write to nigeria/research/NG2-eye-care.md.

NG3, education of blind children. Schools for blind children in Oyo State and the southwest (government and mission), including any school attached to the Federal College of Education (Special) in Oyo; inclusive education policy and practice; braille availability and cost; enrollment, out-of-school blind children, and what keeps them out; what a year of schooling costs a family; boarding; Christian schools for the blind in Nigeria; where the gaps are in Oyo State. Write to nigeria/research/NG3-education.md.

NG4, adult rehabilitation, independent living, and livelihoods. Rehabilitation and vocational centres in Oyo State and Nigeria (federal and state); white cane availability and orientation and mobility training; the reality of begging and destitution among blind adults; livelihood and skills programs (crafts, agriculture, small trade), microfinance and disability inclusion; the Nigeria Association of the Blind and the Joint National Association of Persons with Disabilities in Oyo State; the Discrimination Against Persons with Disabilities Act of 2018 and any Oyo State disability law or agency; what works at small scale. Write to nigeria/research/NG4-rehabilitation-and-livelihoods.md.

NG5, assistive technology and digital access. Screen reader use in Nigeria (NVDA, JAWS, TalkBack); who trains blind people in technology (organizations, universities' disability support units including the University of Ibadan, private trainers); DAISY and audio libraries; smartphone and laptop costs and second-hand markets; data and power costs; remote training that already exists into Nigeria or West Africa; employment outcomes for blind people with digital skills; where a remote instructor from the United States could add value and what a local counterpart would need. Write to nigeria/research/NG5-assistive-technology.md.

NG6, faith-based work with blind people. Churches and Christian organizations serving blind people in Nigeria and the southwest: CBM, the Gindiri Material Centre for the Blind and ECWA, Baptist work around Ogbomoso, Catholic work (Pacelli), Anglican and Pentecostal blind ministries, Yoruba audio Bibles (Faith Comes By Hearing and others), Christian Record Services' and Torch Trust's reach into Africa, braille Scripture in Yoruba; whether Nigerian churches in Indianapolis or Indiana exist that could be partners (organizations only); what faith-based models look like on the ground. Write to nigeria/research/NG6-faith-based-work.md.

NG7, how small foreign churches partner well in Nigeria. Models that work at one to five thousand dollars a year: sponsoring surgeries through a provider, sponsoring a child's schooling, supporting one local worker, funding a small community organization, church-to-church partnership, diaspora-led work; documented failures and fraud patterns and how to avoid them; Nigerian legal context for receiving foreign funds (Corporate Affairs Commission registration, the Special Control Unit against Money Laundering registration for nonprofits, bank requirements); accountability practices that are realistic for a volunteer church; how to start with a visit-free relationship. Write to nigeria/research/NG7-partnering-well.md.

## After the passes

The main model writes the needs map: for each layer, what exists in Oyo State, what is missing, what one unit of help costs, and how well it fits the church's people and money, ending with a ranked set of ways the church could help and the trade-offs between them. Then a directory of organizations to contact and an interview guide friends in Ibadan could use. Then a Sonnet verification pass on every number and name in the report, and a Word document.
