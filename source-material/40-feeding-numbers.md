# Feeding at Bethesda: What Two Meals Cost and What a Third Would Add

Prepared for Brian, October 26, 2026, from Bethesda's food email of October 26 and our representative's visit report of October 25. Prices are Lagos and national market figures found online in October 2026; our representative should check them at a market before any of this is used for a gift or an appeal.

## What Bethesda told us

About 282 boarders are fed two meals a day. In a week they eat rice 4 times, beans twice, Indomie noodles 3 times, and semolina 5 times. That is 14 meals, which is exactly two a day for seven days, so the list is complete for the staples. Per month (4 weeks): 8 bags of rice (50 kg), 4 bags of beans (50 kg), 84 cartons of Indomie, and 80 bags of semolina (10 kg).

The quantities fit the headcount. Seven cartons of Indomie is 280 packets, one per boarder. Half a bag of rice per meal is about 90 grams of dry rice each, and four bags of semolina is about 140 grams each: modest portions.

## What that costs now

Rice: 8 bags at about N55,000 to N62,000 (Lagos survey, October 11) comes to N440,000 to N496,000.
Beans: 4 bags at N56,000 to N100,000 (prices vary widely by type and source) comes to N224,000 to N400,000.
Indomie: 84 cartons at N14,000 to N16,500 (Konga and market listings) comes to N1,176,000 to N1,386,000.
Semolina: 80 bags at about N15,000 to N18,500 (Golden Penny Semovita is N18,530 retail) comes to N1,200,000 to N1,482,000.

Total for the staples, two meals a day: about N3.0 million to N3.8 million a month, middle estimate N3.4 million, which is about $2,560 a month at N1,330 to the dollar ($2,290 to $2,830).

That works out to about N60,800 per meal for the whole home, or about N216 per child per meal, roughly 16 US cents. It is a staples-only figure. It does not include oil, pepper, tomatoes, onions, palm oil, fish or meat for stew and soup, seasoning, salt, or cooking gas or firewood, and semolina and rice are not eaten without soup or stew. Those commonly add a third to a half again. So the real cost of feeding is likely N4.5 million to N5 million a month, about $3,400 to $3,800.

## What a third meal would add

A third daily meal is 7 more meals a week, half again what they cook now. Using the same foods:

Staples only: about N1.7 million a month, roughly $1,280 (range $1,140 to $1,420).
With soup, stew, oil, and fuel: about N2.3 million to N2.5 million a month, roughly $1,700 to $1,900.
Per year: about $15,000 (staples only) to $23,000 (everything).

Per child, a third meal costs about $4.50 to $6.50 a month. That is the clearest number for anyone here: about $5 to $7 a month restores a third meal for one blind child at Bethesda.

A third meal need not cost the same as the others. If it is a lighter breakfast (pap and akara, bread and tea, or garri), it may cost half as much. Bethesda should be asked what the third meal used to be.

## What this means for "alone or with help"

A full third meal, at $1,700 to $1,900 a month, is several times what Brian has been able to put toward Nigeria so far and should not be promised by one person. Partial goals are within reach. $300 a month buys roughly one third meal on weekends (Saturday and Sunday). $640 a month buys staples for a third meal about half the days of the week. Or 30 to 60 people giving $5 to $7 a month restore a third meal for 30 to 60 children. Feeding is a recurring cost that never ends, unlike canes or slates, so it needs a recurring funding base: a giving group, a church, or a US charity with a Nigeria channel (see 26-us-avenues). Nothing should be promised to Bethesda until a source is in hand, and the first step could be a single month as a trial with receipts.

## Questions to send back, through our representative or by email

What did the third meal used to be, and when did it stop? Is the 282 boarders figure the number fed, or do staff and day students also eat? What else is bought each month besides these four staples (oil, soup and stew ingredients, gas), and at what cost? Where do they buy, and at what prices, so we can check against our estimates? Is there any regular donor for food now, and what would happen if a gift stopped after a year? Would food bought in bulk at a wholesale market by them, with receipts, be the preferred form of help?
