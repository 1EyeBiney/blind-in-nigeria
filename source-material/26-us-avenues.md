# Established Avenues in the United States: Could the Church Work Through Someone Else?

Prepared for Brian, September 25, 2026, from two research passes (files U1 and U2 in the research folder) plus the eye-surgery findings already in the cataract note. The question was whether established American organizations already do what the church is considering, so that Brian's job could be gathering people locally and pointing them at those organizations, and lending his technical skills, rather than building anything of his own.

## The short answer

For two of the four things the church has talked about, established American channels exist and are ready today. For the other two, nothing ready-made was found, and the honest conclusion is that a small direct relationship, with a US charity holding the money, is the realistic path.

Cataract surgery: yes, ready today. CharityVision (Vision Outreach International) has a live Nigeria-designated program with a "Donate to Nigeria" option, 1,019 surgeries in 2024; HelpMeSee co-founded the Ibadan training centre. Details are in the cataract note.

Money to a vetted Nigerian organization serving blind people: yes, ready today, through GlobalGiving, which hosts individually vetted Nigerian projects (two live ones support blind adults who beg in Nigeria) and lets a church give tax-deductible, designated dollars with no due-diligence burden. The National Christian Foundation's international granting program is the right long-term vehicle once the church has chosen a specific Nigerian partner, since NCF vets the recipient and handles the cross-border grant.

Scripture and materials for blind people: yes. Lutheran Braille Workers ships free braille and large-print Scripture worldwide to blind people and the agencies that serve them; Talking Bibles International puts audio Bibles in the hands of blind and non-reading people and is explicitly evangelistic; Global Recordings Network has audio Scripture in Yoruba, Hausa, and Igbo. None of this costs the church anything beyond making the request, and any of it could go to Pacelli, Bethesda, or the association in Ibadan tomorrow.

Screen-reader training for blind Nigerians by a remote volunteer: no. Hadley offers free remote courses to learners in more than a hundred countries, and Brian could point Nigerian learners to it or ask whether Hadley takes volunteer facilitators, but no organization was found that places a remote instructor with blind learners in Nigeria one-to-one. If Brian wants to do that, he would be building it, not joining it. That is also the thing he is uniquely equipped to do.

Laptops, canes, and braille supplies to Nigeria: partly. World Computer Exchange refurbishes donated computers and ships them to partner schools in developing countries, historically including West Africa, but whether it has a current Nigeria partner and would preload NVDA needs a phone call. Computers for the Blind (Texas) ships only to US applicants. The National Federation of the Blind sells a carbon-fiber folding cane at $50 with no posted bulk price, and its free cane program's page could not be read, so whether canes can go abroad needs a call to Baltimore. Ambutech sells only through resellers. On the Nigerian side, no purpose-built white canes were found in mainstream retail, which suggests canes are one of the few things worth sending rather than buying locally, but that is inference from thin listings, not a price. No braille slate, stylus, or paper prices were found on either side.

Sponsoring blind students in Nigeria through a US charity: no. Compassion International does not work in Nigeria. No US organization runs a blind-student sponsorship program for Nigeria. The MTN Foundation scholarship for blind students (about ₦300,000) is a Nigerian corporate program that Pacelli's or Bethesda's students could apply to directly. Hope for the Blind Nigeria, a Christian charity in Kaduna State, does braille and computer training and education sponsorship, but Kaduna is far from Ibadan.

Indiana: no Indiana organization or church with a Nigeria blindness program was found. Indiana Lions Clubs support the Indiana School for the Blind and run eyeglass collection; a local club would be a natural ally for collecting items, and Lions clubs exist in Nigeria as a receiving end.

## What this means for Brian's role

There are three ways the church can move money or goods to Nigeria without starting a charity, and all three are ordinary. It can give to an established US charity's designated project (CharityVision for surgery, GlobalGiving for a vetted Nigerian NGO); it can give through a donor-advised or pass-through fund that vets the recipient (National Christian Foundation); or it can put its own project under a fiscal sponsor, a US charity that legally owns the project and receives the gifts while the church runs the work. The IRS rule underneath all of these is that a US charity must keep real control over how the money is spent; a gift earmarked for a named person abroad is not deductible, and a church cannot simply collect money and pass it through. Church Law and Tax and the ECFA both publish plain guidance on this.

So Brian does not need to found anything. A realistic shape: cataract surgeries funded through CharityVision or, after a quote, directly to Eleta under the church's overseas-gift agreement; a first gift to Pacelli or Bethesda routed through the National Christian Foundation or GlobalGiving once one of them is chosen; braille and audio Scripture requested from Lutheran Braille Workers and Talking Bibles at no cost; canes collected locally with the Lions and sent in a December shipment friends in Ibadan are already making, after one call to the NFB; and the remote screen-reader teaching done by Brian himself, one learner at a time, which no organization does and which needs no money to start.
